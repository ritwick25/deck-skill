## Use when
- The firm is introduced in facts, so scale figures, workforce split, reach and recognition have to read in one look.
- Sector breadth or a body of recognition is the claim, shown as marks grouped under sector or year headers.

## Avoid when
- The proof is tied to one engagement and its result, so use case-study instead.
- The slide argues reasons to choose the vendor rather than stating its facts, so use why-us instead.

## Components
- Dominant repeat of tiles, pills or marks, 4 (2 to 6) items at 22% (4 to 95) of the canvas width, with a gutter of 3% (1 to 13).
- Icons 12 (6 to 19) per slide, one beside each scale figure or above each sector tab.
- Pictures 14 (8 to 32) per slide, most of them marks, badges or location photos.
- Connectors 0 (0 to 12) per slide, as dashed ticks, dividers or separators between figures, regions and columns.

## Density
Content words 108 (23 to 226) across 5 exemplars, title words 10 (6 to 22).

## Word budget
Budget 150 words normalized, 170 on the w13 canvas and 140 on the w10 canvas, advisory floor 90, under the ceiling of 250.

## Device notes
- Each scale figure pairs an icon with a large numeral and a short label, the pairs set in one band, strip or grid.
- The stat band runs the full content width directly under the title, and the evidence panels sit below it.
- Marks and badges sit in rows or columns under a tab or header bar that names the sector or the kind of recognition.
- Dashed rules or ticks separate the figures, the regions or the mark columns.
- Reach is shown by place, a map over region columns or a row of office locations grouped by country.

## Translation under the house rules
| Rule | Reference | House | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| no-footer | A confidentiality line, page number, foot logo lockup or navigation roundel closes the slide on 5 top exemplars. | No footer, page number, confidentiality line or foot summary band. | The foot rows are cut and the mark wall or footprint takes the height. |
| first-person | The title or band labels open with a possessive on 3 top exemplars. | No first or second person. | Band labels name their content, such as footprint, partners or expertise, and the title states the fact in the third person. |
| 8pt-floor | Award captions, axis ticks, card labels or a parked off-canvas panel run below the floor on 3 top exemplars. | Every run at 8 pt or above. | Free captions and range brackets are cut, labels grow to the floor, and off-canvas panels are deleted. |
| glyphs | En dashes in ranges, arrow glyphs or smart quotes in captions on 3 top exemplars. | No em dash, en dash, arrow glyph or smart quote in text. | Ranges read with the word to and captions point by position, so stat labels need room for a longer line. |
| shadow | Soft shadows sit under icon roundels or logo tiles on 2 top exemplars. | Hairlines with at most a 1 px shadow. | Roundels become flat discs and tiles take a hairline outline only. |
| flat-fill | A radial gradient fills the stat band on 1 top exemplar. | Flat fills, no gradients. | The band takes one flat brand fill or a light ground with hairline dividers. |
| left-accent | A gradient left edge bar marks no state on 1 top exemplar. | An accent bar only where it encodes a state that can change. | The bar goes and the title band stands alone. |
| no-subtitle | A bold tagline sits under the title on 1 top exemplar. | No subtitle under the title, which is one claim of 14 words or fewer. | The tagline folds into the title keywords and the stat strip moves up. |
| other | Changes outside the named rules appear on 5 top exemplars, among them client and third-party marks, award badges, city photos and titles past the word limit. | No client or outside brand mark travels, and the title is one claim of 14 words or fewer. | Mark walls become sector and scale descriptors in plain labelled pills, the office row becomes a location list, and titles are cut to one line. |
