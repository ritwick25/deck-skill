# Lifecycle loop

| Archetype | Family | Message shape | Taxonomy |
|---|---|---|---|
| a35 | f07 | cycle | v1 |

## Use when
- The work repeats and improves: stages run in order and the last one feeds the first again.
- The argument is that a process never ends, such as a model lifecycle, a learning loop or a mitigation cycle.
- Each stage needs a short label and the loop must still read in one glance.

## Avoid when
- The parts around the center are peers with no order: use a34 hub-and-spoke.
- Many feeders converge onto one shared element and read out to outcomes: use a36 convergence-hub.

## Components
- One closed loop: wedges ringing a hub disc, a segmented wheel, a ring of nodes, a looping arc or step cards with a return line from last to first.
- Direction drawn on the loop itself: curved arrows, chained arrows or one elbow return line.
- Optional stage satellites outside the loop, each tied to one stage: levers, artifacts or exception callouts.
- Optional side panels or a rail flanking the loop, listing what sustains it.
- Optional full-width bands above or below the loop for controls, foundation or measures.
- Icons per slide 8 (0 to 16), connectors per slide 4 (0 to 9).

## Word budget
Word budget 250 pooled, 90 on the standard canvas and 250 on the wide canvas, capped by the 250 ceiling. Advisory floor 80.

## Device notes
- Direction is drawn on the loop, by curved arrows between wedges, arrows between step cards or one return line, so the cycle never depends on reading order.
- Stages share one shape and one size around the loop, wedges or nodes or cards, so no stage reads as the start.
- A solid hub disc in a contrasting fill sits inside wheel loops and names what the cycle serves.
- Supporting content sits outside the loop, in satellites, flanking panels or bands, never inside the stage shapes beyond a short label.

## Translation under the house rules
| Rule | Reference | House | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| no-footer | Confidentiality line, client wordmark, page number, owner strip or a gradient foot band, 5 flagged. | No foot chrome, owner and gate details move to speaker notes. | The loop and bands can use the full height down to the bottom margin. |
| no-subtitle | A sentence line or italic line under the title, 3 flagged. | Delete it, the claim folds into the title or the notes. | The loop moves up into the freed band. |
| left-accent | A gradient left edge bar and colored ticks beside rail headings that encode no state, 3 flagged. | Remove the edge bar, a hairline above each rail heading. | No change to geometry beyond reclaiming the edge strip. |
| flat-fill | Multi-stop gradient edge and foot bars, 2 flagged. | Remove the bars rather than flatten them, flat fills everywhere else. | None on the loop. |
| first-person | Titles and rail headings written as our and we, 2 flagged. | Third person claim, neutral noun headings. | None on layout. |
| word-budget | Card bodies, a how block that restates the ring and cadence descriptions over the budget, 2 flagged. | Card bodies at two lines, restating blocks cut, within the word budget. | A band or panel usually drops, which gives the loop more room. |
| glyphs | Bullet characters in the lever lists, 1 flagged. | Each item set as its own short line. | None on layout. |
| 8pt-floor | Rotated axis labels below the floor, 1 flagged. | Every run at or above 8 pt, or the label is cut where bars already carry direction. | Rotated labels go, so the phase bars must carry their own names. |
| other | Vendor names in a tooling band, a keyword and hyphen title opener, rotated segment labels, floating satellites with no tie to a stage, 5 flagged. | Capability categories in place of vendors, one claim with the keyword in the brand color, horizontal labels, a leader that touches both the satellite and its stage. | Satellites need clear lanes from their stage, which caps how many fit around the loop. |
