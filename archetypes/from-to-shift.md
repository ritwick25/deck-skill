# From-to shift

| Archetype | Family | Message shape | Taxonomy |
|---|---|---|---|
| a23 | f05 | before-after | v1 |

## Use when
- The proposal changes how one subject works, and the audience must see the current and target forms of that same subject drawn with the same parts.
- The change is the argument, so the slide carries the shift itself as a wedge, a bridge of steps or a row of paired cards rather than as a list of benefits.

## Avoid when
- The alternatives have no current state and the choice is between them; use options-comparison instead.
- The slide sets what exists against what is missing rather than one subject in two states; use gap-analysis instead.
- Only the current process is drawn, step by step, with its pains; use as-is-process instead.
- The change unfolds across dated phases or waves; use phased-roadmap instead.

## Components
- Paired state cards or step cards, 5 (4 to 6) items in the dominant repeat, each current item facing its target counterpart.
- Connectors 6 (0 to 42) per slide, from one arrow per paired row to the wiring inside each state diagram.
- Icons 5 (0 to 22) per slide, role, step or row glyphs in one line family that repeat in both states.

## Density
Content words 125 (26 to 252) across 5 top exemplars, with 27 (1 to 39) text shapes per slide.

## Word budget
Budget 190 words normalized and 100 on the w10 canvas, advisory floor 120, under the ceiling of 250.

## Device notes
- A filled tab or header band names each state above its panel, row or card column, so the two states read as labelled columns or bands before any content.
- Both states are drawn with the same parts on the same grid, the same roles, phases, rows or step columns, so each item compares directly with its counterpart.
- The gap between the states carries the change itself: a solid wedge, one arrow per paired row, a chain of steps under a tab to tab arrow, or a band of fixes on the shared step grid.
- A small key decodes the line styles or markers used across both states, so a dashed, solid or colored line keeps one meaning on each side.

## Translation under the house rules
| Rule | Reference | House | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| no-footer | A confidentiality line, wordmark, page number, foot sentence or foot claim band appears on 4 top exemplars. | No footer, page number, confidentiality line or foot summary band. | The closing sentence moves into the title or the speaker notes, and the two states take the freed height. |
| other | Titles run past the word limit, kickers sit above the title, a raster holds the whole body or a mid chart legend carries identity by hue on 5 top exemplars. | A title of one claim in at most 14 words, no kicker, native shapes, and identity by label plus mark rather than hue alone. | Each state line or column is labelled at its end, and the body is rebuilt as editable shapes on the shared grid. |
| 8pt-floor | Legend, rail and chip text renders below the floor on 2 top exemplars. | Type at or above 8 pt everywhere. | Chips drop to one short phrase and partner captions go, so the remaining labels can rise to the floor without crowding. |
| flat-fill | Gradient header bands, a gradient target pill or a gradient edge strip appear on 2 top exemplars. | Flat fills, no gradients. | State headers take one flat brand fill, and the edge strips are removed rather than flattened. |
| left-accent | A short decorative edge bar sits beside the title on 2 top exemplars. | A colored left or top accent bar only where it encodes a changeable state. | Remove the bar, and the title band starts at the margin. |
| shadow | Soft shadows lift the state panels and paired cards on 2 top exemplars. | Hairlines with at most a 1 px shadow. | Keep the hairline outlines that already separate the two states and drop the effects. |
| word-budget | Node notes, chips and fix bodies push the text over budget or below legibility on 2 top exemplars. | Per-archetype word budget under the ceiling of 250. | Each node, step or card keeps one short line, so every paired item holds the same count. |
| no-subtitle | An italic reading line sits under the title on 1 top exemplar. | No subtitle under the title. | Drop the italic line, since it is a reading instruction, and move the context strip up. |
| first-person | A possessive opener in the title and a first-person question in a state panel appear on 1 top exemplar. | No first or second person. | Restate the title as a third-person claim about the shift and turn panel questions into plain state labels. |
