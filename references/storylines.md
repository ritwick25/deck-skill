# Storylines

## How the counts work
Every figure counts lineage-representative decks: 16 of 18 filed decks, with each lineage of cuts counted once under its primary. The flagship lineage's primary is typed workshop, so its short proposal cuts drop out of the proposal counts (5 counted of 7 filed). Multi-deck types: proposal n 5, pov n 4, rfp-response n 2. The rfi-response, capability, workshop and one-pager types each rest on one deck, so every pattern stated for them is single-deck. A share is the share of that type's decks carrying the role or archetype. A position runs from the first main slide to the last. Archetype shares count use anywhere in the deck, so the section an archetype carries comes from the storyline map, not from the share.

## The shared spine
Across all types, the roles present in half the decks or more run in this order by median first position: cover, agenda, divider, problem, credentials, solution, insight, architecture, how-it-works, plan, closing (n 16). A deck runs 24 (1 to 104) main slides over 11 (1 to 21) sections. Problem comes before solution in 63% of the decks carrying both, and credentials before solution in 71%. Dividers appear in 63% of decks and take 14% of main slides. Layout variety holds through the run: 82% of three-slide content windows carry two archetype families or more, while 37% of adjacent content pairs share a family. House rendering: build to the rule rather than the reference average, so any run of consecutive slides mixes layout families and no adjacent pair looks alike.

## Opening moves
The cover (a01) opens 88% of decks and precedes the agenda in 100% of the decks carrying both. An agenda (a02) appears in 56% and lands at 4% (2 to 8) of the run. Credentials arrive at 16% (4 to 100) in the decks that carry them, and an insight or executive summary at 23% (8 to 71). The second beat varies: an executive summary (a28) that states objective, scope and approach before any detail, the problem framed first, or the firm's credentials first. A welcome slide naming the audience before the cover, a team roster before the agenda, and a letter-style perspective before the agenda are each single-deck. House rendering: the cover carries one title and no subtitle line, the perspective is written without first or second person, and the ground stays light.

## Closing moves
A closing (a04) appears in 75% of decks at 98% (85 to 100) of the run. Commercials precede the closing in 100% of the decks carrying both. An appendix appears in 25% of decks at 97% (92 to 100), after the close. Reference closers pair a thank-you lockup with contact details, sometimes a client quotation or office addresses, sometimes on a dark ground. A hidden appendix of sample outputs reached from in-deck links is single-deck, as is an explicit ask slide (a12). House rendering: the closer sits on a light ground, its title states the next step or decision sought, the words Thank you are the only sign-off line, and nothing on it is a footer, page number or foot band.

## Proposal: section order
Order by median first position, n 5: cover, agenda, divider, solution, proof, how-it-works, architecture, plan, commercials, closing. A proposal runs 25 (6 to 69) main slides over 11 (5 to 21) sections. The cover (a01, 100%) and agenda (a02, 80%) open it, and dividers (a03) split parts in 60%. The solution arrives early, at 19% (13 to 31), so the answer leads and the detail follows. Credentials, when present (40%), come right after the agenda at 9% (6 to 11). A separate problem section appears in only 40%, so the restated need usually lives inside the summary. The solution precedes architecture in 100% of decks carrying both, architecture precedes the plan in 75%, and the plan precedes commercials in 100%.

## Proposal: archetypes per section
Summary and solution: executive summary a28 (60%) or input, process, output a15 (60%). Architecture: solution architecture a21 (80%) or layered architecture a20 (40%). How it works: product screen a44 (60%) with a hub and spoke a34 (40%). Plan: Gantt a37 (60%) with a governance model a22 (40%). Credentials and proof: credentials a43 (40%), why us a07 (40%) and a case wall a11 (40%). Commercials: a42 (60%), present in 60% of proposals at 88% (77 to 90) of the run and always before the close (100%). Proof appears in 60% and precedes the close in 100%. Close: a04 (100%).

## POV: section order and archetypes
Order, n 4: cover, divider, problem, insight, solution, architecture, how-it-works, plan, closing, over 25 (5 to 34) main slides. The problem appears in 100% at 11% (3 to 25), as problem framing a05 (50%) or a from-to shift a23 (100%). An insight follows the problem in 100% of decks carrying both, as a convergence hub a36 or an options comparison a24. Architecture follows the problem in 100%, carried by layered architecture a20 (75%) and process flow a13 (75%). How it works is the longest section, 35% of main slides, carried by a worked example a18 (50%), and a swimlane hand-off a17 (50%). The plan (roadmap a38, maturity model a40, Gantt a37) precedes the close in 100%. No POV carries commercials, and few carry an agenda (25%) or credentials (25%). Dividers mark parts in 75%. House rendering: a divider names the part's claim, never an act label.

## RFP response: section order
Order, n 2: cover, team, agenda, insight, divider, problem, context, solution, how-it-works, architecture, credentials, plan, proof, commercials, closing, over 33 (17 to 49) main slides. Each deck opens with an agenda (100%) followed by a divider (100%), and the core runs solution, how it works, architecture, plan, each step in that order in 100%, 100% and 100% of decks. How it works takes 33% of main slides: one canvas per requested item or workstream (input, process, output a15, decision logic a16, swimlane a17, engagement model a19). Credentials float, first appearing at 59% (19 to 100) of the run. Single-deck: a team roster before the agenda, an executive summary a28 right after it, a paired approach page and deliverables page per workstream, and a commercials divider standing in for a separate price volume.

## RFP response: follow the request's order
The library holds the responses, not the source requests, so fidelity to a request's section order is not measured. What the decks show is the mechanism. One restates the client's expectations, selection timeline and requested items before answering, and its contents menu returns as the divider before each part (single-deck). The other numbers its agenda by the response sections and opens each with a photo divider (single-deck). Guidance for a response: the agenda lists the request's own section headings in the request's order, each divider repeats that heading, each requested item gets its own canvas in the order the request lists it, and material the request did not ask for (credentials, team, extra capability) sits where the request places it or after the requested sections, ahead of the close. House rendering: the returning divider is a light-ground agenda with the active part marked, which is a changeable state and so may carry the accent bar.

## RFI response (single-deck)
Single-deck, n 1. Order: cover, context, agenda, credentials, divider, architecture, solution, proof, problem, insight, commercials, team, closing. It opens with an ask-versus-response summary as an executive summary a28 placed before the agenda (100%), then an agenda tracker that returns before each part. Credentials come early (credentials a43, hub and spoke a34, case wall a11), then the answer: layered architecture a20 with a coverage matrix a31 and a reference table a33 for the catalogue. Problem framing arrives late, as a gap analysis a25, followed by an insight carried by a prioritization matrix a32. Commercials pair a coverage matrix a31 with why us a07, then a team roster a10 and the close a04. House rendering: the closing quotation stays only as a quotation, office addresses go, and the tracker marks the active part as its state.

## Capability deck (single-deck)
Single-deck, n 1, 13 (13 to 13) main slides. Order: cover, problem, context, architecture, insight, credentials, solution, plan, team, case, closing. It argues from the market inward: problem framing a05 opens straight after the cover, context and insight reuse the same framing archetype, layered architecture a20 places the offer on the client's existing stack, a maturity model a40 carries the credentials, a use-case map a30 and a catalogue a09 carry the solution, and a phased roadmap a38 carries the plan. The problem precedes the solution (100%). Team, then a case wall a11, then the close a04 end it, so proof sits at the end rather than the front.

## Workshop and the flagship lineage
The workshop type is single-deck and is the flagship lineage's primary: a long master counted once, with its short proposal cuts filed under it and left out of the proposal counts. The master runs 104 (104 to 104) main slides in 19 (19 to 19) sections. Order: cover, agenda, divider, credentials, team, context, solution, insight, architecture, how-it-works, plan, proof, case, ask, problem, closing, appendix, other. It holds an arc that sells the firm and the field: welcome and cover, agenda, credentials and team, industry context, a use-case catalogue (use-case map a30, offering pillars a08), capability deep dives, and a target architecture (a20, a21). A later arc solves a single problem: problem framing, the proposed solution, a workflow walkthrough, a product walkthrough, design principles, a delivery roadmap (a38, a37) and governance (a22), then next steps and the close. A divider opens every section (15% of main slides) and how it works takes 21%. The short cuts keep only the second arc: value horizons, an agent roadmap, a before and after, one deep dive, architecture, plan, proof and the close.

## Flagship: backup and graveyard after the close
After next steps and the close, the master keeps a backup run of case studies (case study a26), then an appendix divider (closing precedes appendix in 100%), then a graveyard of retired slides behind it (appendix precedes the graveyard in 100%). Single-deck. House rendering: backup case studies stay after the close under one appendix divider, and the graveyard never ships. It lives in a separate working file, because graveyard slides and anything revealing edit history are structural tells to strip. Reference dividers are full-bleed grayscale photos with a number badge, and the reference content shell carries a gradient bar at the left edge and the foot, a wordmark and a confidentiality line. House rendering: a light-ground divider carrying the section claim, flat fills, and no foot band, footer, wordmark strip or page number.

## Reference habits the house renders differently
Devices are borrowed, rules win. Type never drops below 8 pt in any run. Grounds are light, including dividers and closers that the references set on dark photos. Fills are flat, never gradient bars or gradient bands. Card edges are hairlines with a shadow of at most 1 px. No footer, page number, confidentiality line or foot band. No subtitle under a title, and an action title is one claim of at most 14 words. No act labels on dividers. No em dash, en dash or arrow glyph in text, and drawn arrows touch both shapes. No first or second person in deck voice. A coloured bar no thicker than 0.10 in appears only where it encodes a state that can change, such as the active part on a returning agenda tracker.

## One-pager (single-deck)
Single-deck, n 1, on the A4 portrait canvas a4p. The page stacks the storyline in bands rather than slides: a title stating the offer, a context band, side-by-side columns setting the current process against the opportunity and its challenges, a framed workflow that drops the AI step into the human review loop, numbered modules in run order, and an annotated sample output. The whole page is carried by one executive summary archetype a28 (100%). House rendering: the header band is a flat fill, not an accent bar, and the 8 pt floor holds on the densest band.
